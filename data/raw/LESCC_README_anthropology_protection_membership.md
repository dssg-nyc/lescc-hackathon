# LESCC: Anthropology, Protection and Membership

This README explains `lescc_anthropology_protection_membership.json`. The companion file is `lescc_hub_model.json` (the bid and revenue model).

## The idea in brief

LESCC's plan creates value in the neighborhood. This file sets out three connected parts that keep that value from displacing the people who create it:

1. **Anthropology.** A community-owned living archive of the neighborhood's cultures, recorded by paid community ethnographers.
2. **Protection.** Part of the value the plan creates goes, in advance, to protecting residents, legacy businesses and artists. Promotion only expands when protection is funded and the displacement indicators are stable.
3. **Membership.** A two-sided district partnership in which social members and capital members have equal standing and protection is a core purpose.

## Why it exists

Culture raises business in the area, and that same rise in value drives up rents and eviction pressure. Displaced residents don't disappear: their costs move elsewhere in the city as shelter, health care, lost jobs and broken support networks. A standard Business Improvement District (BID) speeds this up. Property owners pay in and control the board, residents hold token seats, and success means higher rents.

Community District 3 has the widest gap between its lowest and highest earners of NYC's 59 community districts. Within 400 m of the site, 21% of residents are below the poverty line, 30% of households include a senior, and 24% of households have limited English.

## 1. Anthropology

- **Communities:** Chinese (about 8,900 residents within 400 m; no representation yet), Loisaida (Puerto Rican and Dominican), Jewish heritage, artists, youth, elders living alone, and local food makers, treated as cultural practitioners rather than only as vendors.
- **Paid community ethnographers**, at least one per community. They record oral histories, a cultural calendar, a map of places where culture is practiced, foodways, and a register of legacy businesses.
- **Uses:** decides what the hub programs, provides the lists of legacy businesses and at-risk people for protection, and provides public-value evidence for the bid.
- **Ownership of the records:** each community owns its records, and LESCC only holds them in trust. Consent can be revoked, and records are never used for real-estate marketing. Everything is in English, Cantonese, Mandarin and Spanish.

## 2. Protection

- **Cultural Continuity & Anti-Displacement Fund:** funded by a share of every capital pledge and of hub sales. It pays for:
  - emergency rent-arrears grants;
  - lease support for legacy businesses;
  - stipends for cultural practitioners;
  - land-trust acquisitions.
- **Tenant help desk:** multilingual referrals to Right to Counsel, rent-stabilization checks, HPD and legal aid.
- **Early-warning system:** watches individual buildings for eviction filings, housing code violations, construction permits, sales and storefront closures, and triggers outreach when they appear.
- **Permanent affordability:** partnership with the Cooper Square Community Land Trust (the oldest active land trust in NYC, on the LES) and support for the HDFC co-ops on the block.
- **Good-neighbor pledge:** capital members commit to no tenant harassment and fair lease renewals for legacy businesses.
- **Policy asks:** anti-harassment certification (CONH) coverage, Council money for legal services, and a Park Improvement District pilot that includes a protection share.
- **Coupling rule (the core innovation):** promotion grows only as fast as protection. If the fund misses its target or the indicators get worse for two quarters, the cultural council can pause promotion and send surplus money to the fund.
- **The city-level case:** family shelter costs about $270 a day, compared with about $54 for a CityFHEPS rental voucher (Win NYC, Feb 2026). The general fund that receives Parks' fees also pays for shelter, so protection saves money for the same budget.

**Baseline: executed evictions**

| | 400 m | 800 m |
|---|---|---|
| Residential 2023 / 2024 / 2025 | 21 / 14 / 10 | 75 / 59 / 49 |
| Commercial 2023 / 2024 / 2025 | 3 / 3 / 4 | 18 / 15 / 12 |

Executed evictions undercount displacement: they leave out informal moves, buyouts and rent-driven departures.

## 3. Membership

### Why capital members join
They're buying into the district's distinctiveness, and displacement erodes it. The anchor example is Nine Orchard (MML Hospitality, Austin), bought for $92M in August 2025 as MML's first East Coast project. A hotel that sells a living Lower East Side has a business interest in keeping it alive.

### Two member classes

| | Capital members | Social members |
|---|---|---|
| Who | Hotels, property owners, larger businesses | Residents, tenant associations, HDFC co-ops, legacy businesses, cultural groups, congregations, artists |
| What they contribute | Cash pledges, scaled to capacity (anchor, major, neighborhood tiers) | Time, programming, cultural and ethnographic work, counted in time-credits |
| What they get | A stable, distinctive district, referrals from the hub, data on uplift, event tie-ins | Protection fund, tenant help desk, a turn in the rotating storefront, paid cultural work, community-owned records |

Every member is a full member regardless of how much they pledge. Time-credits carry full governance rights.

### Order in which money is spent
1. Continuity & Anti-Displacement Fund (the protection share comes off the top)
2. Guarantee of Parks' fee for the Allen Street concession
3. Hub operations and stipends for community ethnographers
4. District programs and marketing (only expand when protection indicators allow)

*Decision for the team:* this order puts protection ahead of Parks' fee.

### Staging

| Stage | Form | Needs legislation? |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Voluntary nonprofit partnership with signed member agreements | No |
| 2 | Pledges guarantee LESCC's bid for the concession (check with Ian McDermott) | No |
| 3 | LES BID sub-district or Park Improvement District pilot, with the protection share built in | Yes |

## Governance

- **Cultural council:** residents hold the majority. It governs the archive and decides how the protection fund is used, including pausing promotion.
- **District board:** 50% capital members and 50% social members, with a chair that rotates each year.
  - Protection decisions need approval from a resident majority of the social half.
  - Marketing and the budget need approval from both halves.
  - Changing the governance rules needs a supermajority of each half, voting separately.
- **Protections against capture by capital members:**
  - Voting power doesn't increase with pledge size.
  - The rules are written into the founding documents before the first pledge is accepted.
  - No member or group holds more than one seat.
  - An annual public report shows pledges, spending, protection metrics and economic uplift side by side.
- **Open item:** merge the cultural council and the district board into one body, or keep two.

## Approach

Relationships first, and no litigation. Nine Orchard is treated as a prospective anchor member and partner, not as an opponent.

## How it answers the RFP's scoring criteria

| Criterion | Answer |
|---|---|
| Operator experience | Partner organizations' track record, including the Cooper Square CLT |
| Pricing | Free and low-cost access set by the poverty and limited-English data |
| Capital investment | Restored building plus the living archive as a public asset |
| Design | Co-designed with documented communities |
| Sustainability | Cultural and residential continuity, measured every quarter |

## Reporting rule

Economic uplift is never reported without the protection metrics next to it.

## Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Capital members take over governance | Equal halves and the protections above, fixed before the first pledge |
| Pledges shrink after year one | Multi-year agreements, annual uplift reports, stage 3 as a backstop |
| Social contributions are undervalued | Time-credits carry full governance rights |
| Success speeds up displacement | Protection share comes first in spending, and promotion is tied to protection |
| Concession rules may not accept a guarantee from a third party | Check early. Fallback: pledges are paid to LESCC as operating revenue |
