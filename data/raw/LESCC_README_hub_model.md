## The idea in brief

LESCC runs the restored Allen Street comfort station as an **LES visitor and culture hub**. It earns money by sending visitors to the neighborhood's cultural groups and businesses, not by competing with them. Parks gets a guaranteed fee, a restored building and open public restrooms. Every cultural group gets a sales channel and a turn in the storefront.

## Why the hub

- **Food is saturated.** There are 321 food businesses within a 5-minute walk, including at least 6 cafés within 130 m of the building. An LESCC café would compete with neighbors.
- **Culture and restrooms are scarce.** There are 36 cultural organizations within a 5-minute walk, and the nearest working public restroom is 220 m away.
- **Parks needs revenue,** and a single food vendor has failed here before: the 2016 RFP produced nothing.

## Revenue streams

1. **Booking desk:** sells tours, tickets and classes for all partner organizations, and lodging at member hotels, for a commission. Each sale is a measurable referral. With hotels, referrals run both ways: hotel concierges sell hub tours and programs to their guests, and the hub sends visitors and visiting artists to member hotels such as Nine Orchard.
2. **Maker shop:** sells local artists' and craftspeople's work on consignment, plus partners' merchandise.
3. **Rotating storefront:** a different cultural group runs the front room each month, and LESCC takes a revenue share.
4. **Rotating food counter:** neighborhood cafés and bakeries take turns running it. This is the formal food-service concession, which keeps the bid within what the RFP asks for.
5. **Evening rentals:** small performances, readings and events.
6. **Public restrooms:** free, with published hours.

## What Parks gets

- **Cash:** a guaranteed minimum annual fee, or a percentage of sales from every stream, whichever is larger.
- **Costs Parks avoids:** restoration capital raised by LESCC, restroom operation, and maintenance of the mall.
- **Lower risk:** the fee is backed by pledges from the coalition.

## The radical part: cooperating fully with the hotel

The LESCC group's first reaction was to treat Nine Orchard (MML Hospitality), which is reportedly proposing food trucks for the site, as an opponent. **Claude suggested the opposite: invite them in as a partner.** The group saw this as radical.

The reasoning:
- MML bought Nine Orchard for $92M in August 2025, its first East Coast project. The hotel sells a living, authentic Lower East Side.
- If the people who make the neighborhood distinctive are displaced, MML's asset loses value. Supporting culture and protecting residents is in their own business interest.
- MML is a restaurant group. It could run a turn at the rotating food counter and become an anchor capital member of the district partnership, pledging toward Parks' fee and the protection fund.
- Partnership avoids a contest over the concession. That fits the team's principle of no litigation: litigation is expensive and unpredictable, and relationships are not.

The team has to decide whether to pursue this, and who would hold the relationship. Any approach should be made in person by a team member.

## How the hub connects to the rest of the plan

- **Anthropology:** the community living archive decides what goes in the storefront and the booking desk.
- **Protection:** a share of hub sales goes to the Cultural Continuity & Anti-Displacement Fund, and promotion only grows as fast as protection.
- **Membership:** capital members, potentially including MML, and social members back the fee guarantee and govern the district together on equal terms.

## Risk: meeting the RFP's requirements

The RFP is for a **food service** concession. A hub with no food could be rejected as not meeting it. The rotating food counter is the formal concession, and the hub is built around it. Ian McDermott should check this.
